Epidemiology & Overview

Heart disease encompasses diverse pathophysiological conditions impairing myocardial structure and function. According to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), cardiovascular disease remains the primary cause of mortality globally, accounting for approximately 1 in every 4 deaths. Early detection of subclinical ischemia and aggressive management of modifiable risk factors drastically mitigate coronary events.

Major Clinical Classifications

Coronary Artery Disease (CAD)

Atherosclerotic plaque accumulation within the epicardial coronary arteries reduces luminal diameter, restricting oxygen delivery to the myocardium and provoking stable angina or acute coronary syndrome.

Cardiac Arrhythmias

Disruptions in cardiac electrical conduction producing tachycardia, bradycardia, premature ventricular beats, or atrial fibrillation that impair cardiac output efficiency.

Myocardial Infarction (Heart Attack)

Acute thrombus occlusion following unstable atherosclerotic plaque rupture, resulting in transmural or subendocardial cardiomyocyte necrosis.

Heart Failure (HF)

Progressive inability of the ventricles to fill or eject adequate volume to meet metabolic demands, often culminating from unmanaged chronic hypertension, ischemia, or cardiomyopathy.

Cardiomyopathy

Primary myocardial structural disease, including dilated cardiomyopathy (ventricular dilation and thinning) and hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (genetic sarcomeric wall thickening).

Valvular Heart Disease

Stenosis, regurgitation, or prolapse affecting aortic, mitral, tricuspid, or pulmonary valves, leading to hemodynamic strain and chamber remodeling.

Symptom Recognition Spectrum

Vascular & Anginal Signs

Substernal chest pressure, tightness, radiating discomfort to left arm, neck, or jaw; exertional dyspnea, unexplained diaphoresis, or epigastric discomfort.

Arrhythmic Signs

Palpitations, sensation of skipped beats, sudden presyncope, dizziness, or lightheadedness occurring with or without exertional triggers.

Congestive Failure Signs

Orthopnea, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea, bilateral peripheral pedal edema, rapid fluid weight gain, and profound exertional fatigue.

Emergency Warning Signs

Immediate 911/emergency evaluation is mandatory for crushing retrosternal chest pain exceeding 5 minutes, acute syncope, or severe resting respiratory distress.

Evidence-Based Risk Reduction

Key modifiable lifestyle factors identified by the American Heart Association (AHA Life's Essential 8):

Blood Pressure Optimization

Target resting systolic < 120 mm Hg and diastolic < 80 mm Hg through sodium restriction, exercise, and targeted pharmacotherapy.

Lipid Profile Management

Maintain optimal LDL-C (< 100 mg/dL or < 70 mg/dL in high risk), non-HDL cholesterol, and triglycerides via diet and lipid-lowering agents.

Glycemic Control

Control fasting blood glucose < 100 mg/dL and HbA1c < 5.7% to avert diabetic micro- and macrovascular endothelial injury.

Aerobic Physical Activity

Engage in at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity or 75 minutes of vigorous-intensity cardiovascular exercise per week.
